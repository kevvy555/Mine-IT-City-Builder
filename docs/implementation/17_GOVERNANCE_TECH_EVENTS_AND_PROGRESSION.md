# 17 — Governance, Technology, Events and Progression

## Player authority

The player is the game-facing **Concordia Metropolitan Steward**.

This is an interface abstraction, not a claim that Universe canon contains an office with exactly that title.

The player has delegated planning/operational authority, not sovereignty.

## Governance state

Track:

- metropolitan mandate;
- district concerns;
- policy set;
- capital projects;
- operating/capital budget;
- consultation/approval state where required;
- institutional trust/reliability metrics.

Mandate is multi-factor and issue-specific.

## District layers

Policies may target:

- whole city;
- planning district;
- service district;
- environmental/heritage district;
- transit district.

Overlapping policy scopes resolve through explicit precedence.

## Policy definition

Policy content includes:

- stable ID;
- scope;
- eligibility;
- activation/deactivation conditions;
- implementation delay;
- operating/capital cost;
- affected domain parameters;
- explanation text;
- conflicts/prerequisites.

No policy directly discriminates by species.

## Policy effects

Policies do not set arbitrary headline outcomes.

Example:

```text
Transit priority policy
→ junction priority parameter
→ lower transit delay
→ changed route choice
→ changed accessibility
→ changed development/household outcomes
```

UI can trace this chain.

## Technology programmes

Year 5300 begins technologically mature.

Progression is modernisation/specialisation, not primitive unlocks.

Programme areas:

- utilities;
- transit;
- construction;
- materials;
- medicine;
- environment;
- automation/AI;
- logistics.

Research consumes:

- institution capacity;
- skilled staff;
- funding;
- time.

## In-world AI constraint

AI systems may support:

- forecasting;
- scheduling;
- maintenance prediction;
- anomaly detection;
- dispatch;
- recommendations.

Strategic civic decisions remain accountable to biological/civic authority consistent with canon.

## Events

Sources:

- simulation thresholds;
- deterministic random event stream;
- calendar;
- scenario;
- wider Commonwealth/external events.

Event effects operate through ordinary systems where possible.

Example:

external supply disruption
→ connector capacity/reliability changes
→ freight delay
→ inventory shortage
→ production/service impact.

## News

News records facts from simulation/history:

- event;
- affected district/entities;
- metrics;
- causes;
- response.

Different narrative voices may frame facts differently but cannot invent contradictory simulation outcomes.

## Progression

Primary progression dimensions:

- jurisdiction;
- mandate;
- fiscal capacity;
- institutional capability;
- infrastructure modernisation;
- city outcomes;
- scenario objectives.

Avoid “reach population X to unlock sewage.”

## Goals

Scenario goals are outcome based:

- reduce commute inequality;
- modernise district energy;
- add housing without displacement;
- improve flood resilience;
- restore fiscal health;
- deliver major transit project.

Goals include measurable success ranges and time windows only where appropriate.

## Failure

Most failure is recoverable:

- fiscal crisis;
- service collapse;
- population loss;
- mandate loss;
- disaster recovery.

Hard fail is reserved for explicit scenario conditions, not ordinary experimentation.

## History

Record important changes:

- projects;
- policies;
- disasters;
- major development;
- landmarks;
- population milestones;
- fiscal crises;
- district transformations.

History powers news and long-term city identity.

## Exit criteria

- policy explains mechanism rather than hidden bonus;
- conflicting policies resolve deterministically;
- technology improves existing systems without false primitive-era gating;
- event effects can be traced through simulation;
- no policy/job/education rule derives productivity from species identity;
- history survives save/load and aggregation.
